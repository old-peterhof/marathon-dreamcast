# Good Boy Gumball

A Game Boy (DMG) game about a dog who is worried he was bad.

---

## 1. The premise

Gumball falls asleep at home.

He wakes on a grassy field beside a patch of fresh-turned soil. The field is
overgrown, wild, unkept. His family is not here. He remembers, in flashes, the
things he did wrong: he bit the Boy, he stole from the Girl's plate, he dug up
the Mother's garden, he barked all night and would not come when the Father
called.

He goes to find them. He is afraid that when he does, they will not say he was a
good boy.

He finds them one at a time. Each one says it.

What Gumball does not know, and what the player works out well before he does, is
that they have been waiting to say it since the morning they found him.

---

## 2. What is actually true

This is a memorial for a real dog, and the design answers to the real facts.

- He died in his sleep, at home.
- Nobody got to say goodbye.
- He was buried in the rain and the mud.

Every rule below exists because of those three lines.

**Nobody in the family has a name.** They are the Boy, the Girl, the Mother and
the Father, because that is what they are to a dog. He never knew their names.
Referring to them by role puts the whole game inside his head instead of
narrating it from outside, and it lets any player's family stand in the same
shape.

The consequence is worth protecting: **Gumball is the only named character in the
game.** His name is in the title, the family are roles, the antagonist is a black
cat. The people who gave him that name are the ones who say it back to him at the
end, and the Father saying it in the rain is the only time in the game a human
speaks it aloud.

**He is not running from anything, and nothing killed him.** There is no chase in
this game that is about his death, no fall, no road, no accident. He went to
sleep. That is the whole of it, and staging a more dramatic death would be a lie
told about someone who cannot correct it.

**The game opens with the last thing that happened to him.** He is asleep on the
title screen. The player's first input wakes him up.

**The wound the game closes is the goodbye, in both directions.** Gumball is
missing "good boy." The family is missing the chance to say it. One reunion fixes
both, four times.

**The rain belongs to the burial and appears nowhere else** until the very last
screen, where it is what grew the flowers.

---

## 3. The three rules

> **Rule one. Every world is built around the exact sin Gumball committed against
> that family member. The mechanic he is ashamed of is the mechanic that saves
> them.**

Put the bad memory in the controller, not in a cutscene.

> **Rule two. The sins are what Gumball did. The bosses are what Gumball was
> afraid of.**

Every boss is a real dog fear, made of that world's material, beaten with that
world's verb. Nothing in this game is a monster. Three are machines and one is
the weather.

> **Rule three. Every reunion answers two things. His fear, and their unfinished
> goodbye.**

He needs to hear that he was good. They need to say the thing they were not in
the room to say. The second half is what the player is really playing for, and
Gumball does not understand it until the end.

---

## 4. The four worlds

Order runs from the child who was told the least to the adult who carried him.

### World 1 — the Boy
**Sin:** he bit him, playing too rough.
**Verb: BITE.** Attack and traversal both. Bite a rope and swing, bite a toy and
carry it, bite a sheet and pull it into a bridge.
**Theme:** the house at a kid's height. Toy blocks, a cushion fort, a hallway a
mile long, the back yard through a screen door.
**His unfinished thing:** nobody explained it to him properly. Children get the
short version.

| Level | Setting | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1-1 | The Boy's bedroom | Learn to bite. Everything soft. |
| 1-2 | Hallway and stairs | Bite-swing. Cat appears. Flashback: the bite. |
| 1-3 | The bathroom | **Boss: THE BATH.** |

> "You bit me because we were playing. You were playing *with* me."
> "They said you went away. Nobody would say where. I looked for ages."

### World 2 — the Girl
**Sin:** he stole food off her plate.
**Verb: CARRY (and resist).** You carry food you are not allowed to eat. A
hunger meter climbs. Eating what you carry heals you *and* fails the objective.
**Theme:** the kitchen and dining room, scaled so Gumball is small. Counters as
platforms, chair legs as pillars, the underside of the table as a ceiling.
**Her unfinished thing:** she was not there. She was at school, or upstairs, or
out. She has never stopped being angry about a thing that was nobody's fault.

| Level | Setting | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 2-1 | The pantry | Learn to carry. Nothing chasing you yet. |
| 2-2 | Countertops and sink | Carry across gaps. Cat appears. Flashback: the plate. |
| 2-3 | Under the dining table | **Boss: THE VACUUM.** |

> "I always gave you some anyway. I just liked when you sat with me."
> "I wasn't there. I've been so angry that I wasn't there."

### World 3 — the Mother
**Sin:** he dug up her garden and tracked mud through the house.
**Verb: DIG.** Destructive and also the only way forward. Buried paths, buried
keys, buried carrots.
**Theme:** the garden gone feral. Overgrown beds, a greenhouse with cracked
panes, a compost heap. This world rhymes visually with the opening field.
**Her unfinished thing:** she was the one who found him. She is the only one who
saw, and she has been carrying that on her own.

| Level | Setting | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 3-1 | The flower beds | Learn to dig. Soft soil everywhere. |
| 3-2 | The greenhouse | Dig under glass. Cat appears. Flashback: the ruined bed. |
| 3-3 | The long grass | **Boss: THE LAWNMOWER.** |

> "I replanted it every spring. I liked having a reason to."
> "You looked like you were still asleep. I stood there a long time before I
> could say anything."

### World 4 — the Father
**Sin:** he barked all night, chewed things that mattered, and would not come
when called.
**Verb: BARK, and the LEASH.** Bark to call things toward you and to find your
way. A leash tether limits your distance from an anchor. In the last stretch the
leash comes off, because there is nothing left to hold you back and nowhere
dangerous to go.
**Theme:** the neighborhood at night, then the back garden, then rain. The only
world with real quiet in it and the only dark palette.
**His unfinished thing:** he dug the hole. In the rain, in the mud. He talked to
Gumball the whole time and got no answer, and he has never known whether it
counted.

| Level | Setting | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 4-1 | The back fence and alley | Learn to bark. Learn the leash. |
| 4-2 | Driveways and parked cars | Bark to navigate. Cat's last appearance. |
| 4-3 | The garden, rain starting | **Boss: THE STORM.** |

> "I wasn't angry. I never once was angry."
> "I sat with you after. I know you couldn't hear me. I said it anyway."

That last line is the one the whole game is built to deliver. Everything else is
setup for it.

---

## 5. The three-level shape

Every world uses the same three-beat template. The player learns the shape once
and then anticipation does half the work.

- **Level 1 — Teach.** Introduce the verb in a safe space. No fear pressure, no
  cat, no failure that costs much. End somewhere warm.
- **Level 2 — Complicate.** The verb gets a second use and a cost. The black cat
  appears here, once. One playable flashback: a short, muted, small-arena scene of
  the actual bad memory, in which the player commits the sin themselves and
  cannot avoid it.
- **Level 3 — Fear.** The world's fear leaks into the level before the boss
  arena: distant mower noise, water across the tiles, a vacuum cord underfoot,
  thunder with no rain yet. Boss at the end. Reunion after.

Do not let the player merely *hear* that he bit the Boy. Make them press the
button.

---

## 6. The bosses

None of them are alive. Three are machines and one is the sky. All are three
phases, one hit per phase, because the reward is the reunion and not the
challenge.

### 1-3 — THE BATH
The tub is filling and will not stop. Suds churn, the shower head sweeps the
room, the tiles are slick.

You cannot bite water. That is the joke and the puzzle. You bite the plug chain,
you bite the towel off the rail to make a raft, you bite the curtain rod so it
falls across the tub. Three bites, three phases, the water drains.

The lightest boss in the game, deliberately. World 1 should make someone laugh.

### 2-3 — THE VACUUM
It is under the dining table with you and it wants the crumbs. It inhales carrots
off the floor, stealing your health while you carry food you are not allowed to
eat.

**The boss is greedier than Gumball ever was.** That is the entire point of it.

Beat it by carrying heavy things into the intake: a bone, a wooden block, a
napkin ring. Three jams and it chokes. Everything it swallowed spills back out.

### 3-3 — THE LAWNMOWER
It cuts lanes through the tall grass you hide in, so the arena shrinks every
pass. It shreds the flower beds as it goes.

**It is doing to the garden what Gumball did, only worse and on purpose.** He dug
up her flowers; this thing erases them. He has to dig to save what is left.

Dig trenches it stalls in, dig up stones that jam the blade. Three jams. When it
stops, the grass stays cut, and the lane it carved is the path to the Mother.

### 4-3 — THE STORM
The rain starts during the level and does not stop.

You cannot hit weather. Lightning telegraphs and then deletes a platform. Wind
pushes. The arena is the back garden and it is turning to mud, and the mud slows
you, and there is a shape in the far corner the camera will not quite let you
look at.

The first time you bark, nothing answers. Silence, held a full two seconds. It is
the only moment in the game where the verb fails.

Then you learn to bark from the right places. The fence post, the shed, the back
step. Each answered bark is the Father's voice, closer than the last, and each
time the rain gets a little quieter rather than louder.

On the third he says Gumball's name. The leash comes off. There is nothing to run
from and no road to cross. You just walk to him through the mud, and it takes a
while, and that is correct.

**No white flash, no thunderclap sting, no cut to black.** The storm simply ends
because he is holding you.

### On restraint
The player understands what the garden is. Gumball does not. Do not have anyone
say it, do not put a headstone in the arena, and do not linger the camera on the
shape in the corner. One tile of turned earth at the edge of the screen is
enough, and it should look exactly like the patch from the opening field, which
is how the player finally puts it together.

---

## 7. The reunion

The most important fifteen seconds in each world, and it is the same ritual every
time. Repetition is what makes it land.

**Beat 1.** The boss stops. All music cuts. Not a fade, a cut. Silence and room
tone.

**Beat 2.** The family member is standing there doing something completely
ordinary. The Boy holding a ball. The Girl holding a plate. The Mother with a
trowel. The Father holding a leash with nothing on the end of it.

**Beat 3.** The player has control. No prompt, no arrow, no button hint. You have
to walk to them yourself. Most players hesitate, and the hesitation is the
design.

> If the player stands still, the family member waits. Forever. They never leave
> and they never call out. That is a statement, and it costs nothing to
> implement.

**Beat 4.** Within about three tiles, control is taken for the **shy-away**:
Gumball stops, ears flatten, head turns aside, tail tucks. He is bracing to be
scolded.

**Beat 5.** The hand comes down. He flinches into it. Pet, then hug, then **two
lines**:

1. The forgiveness. Answers the sin. "You were playing *with* me."
2. The goodbye. Answers the thing they never got to say. This one is not about
   Gumball's guilt at all, and the first time it happens the player realizes the
   game has a second floor underneath the one they were standing on.

Then:

- max carrots +1, and the bar visibly grows
- the idle sprite upgrades a stage
- a short "good boy" motif plays, different for each family member
- **the world theme never plays again**

### Gumball does not understand the second line
Not until world 4. He hears "I wasn't there" and "I stood there a long time" and
takes them as more evidence that he did something wrong. The player is ahead of
him for the entire game. That gap is the whole engine of the thing, and it closes
on the Father's last line.

### The shy-away gets shorter every time
World 1 it is a full second and he almost backs up. World 2 he stops. World 3 he
only ducks his head. World 4 it is one frame of hesitation and then he is already
running.

Four short sprite sequences, and it is the entire character arc.

---

## 8. The black cat

Appears twice per world: once in level 2, once at the door to the boss. Each
appearance shorter than the last.

Hard rules:

1. **It never attacks.** It is never a boss. There is no fight.
2. **It never lies.** Everything it says is true. The cruelty is entirely in the
   framing, which is far more unsettling than a liar and much harder to argue
   with.
3. **It helps you.** It knows where the family members are and it tells you. It
   is not withholding help, it is charging you emotionally for help you need.
4. **It is not defeated.** In the finale it is simply present, and the family
   knows it.
5. **Never confirm whether it is a real cat or Gumball's own guilt.** Both
   readings survive to the credits.

### Its cruelty is about the goodbye, not the sins
The sins are small and the cat knows it. The real wound is the one Gumball cannot
name yet, so that is where the cat pushes.

> "They buried you in a nice spot."
> "Nobody said goodbye to you. You know that, don't you."
> "The Boy still has the scar. I'm only saying what's true."
> "You are looking for people who had to learn to live without you."
> "Do you think they wanted you to find them?"
> "Go on, then. He's just through there." *(before a boss)*

Every one of those is factually true. That is what makes it unbearable.

### Its resolution
The cat has said "nobody said goodbye to you" in every world. At the end it turns
out that the cat has been saying it about itself. It is waiting for someone who
has not come and may not be coming.

The cruelty was never about Gumball.

Nobody explains this. The cat gets one final line, the family reacts to the cat
like they know it, and the player does the arithmetic. Whether Gumball's family
takes the cat with them is the last thing the game shows and the last thing it
says nothing about.

---

## 9. Carrots

Carrots are health, because Gumball loved them.

- The health bar is a row of carrot icons, not hearts.
- A small carrot restores one.
- Hidden golden carrots per level; three in a world unlocks something small and
  warm, not a power.
- **Every forgiveness permanently raises max carrots by one.** Love is literally
  the health bar. Four family members, four extra carrots. Nearly free to
  implement, and the player is measurably stronger for having been forgiven.

---

## 10. The two animation arcs

The highest-value cheap ideas in the whole design, and they reinforce each other.

**The idle sprite.** Starts with tail down, ears flat, head low. After each
forgiveness the idle changes: ears up, head lifts, tail begins to move, and after
the fourth it wags.

**The shy-away.** Shrinks each world, as above, until it is a single frame.

A handful of tiles each. No dialogue. Every player notices and no player needs it
explained.

---

## 11. The opening and the ending

### Opening
The title screen is Gumball asleep in his bed at home. Not a menu illustration,
the actual game engine, actual sprite, breathing.

The player presses a button. He wakes up in the field.

The first input in the game is the last thing that happened to him, and the game
never mentions it.

### Ending
No final boss. The last map is a walk back across the field. One at a time each
family member falls in behind you until all four are walking with you. The four
"good boy" motifs layer in as they join, so the finale theme assembles itself out
of the four reunions.

The patch of soil is gone, covered in flowers. It rained here, once, hard, and
this is what came of it. That is the only time the rain is allowed to mean
something good and it is the last image in the game.

The last line is the title. Fade.

After the credits the title screen has changed: he is asleep in his bed and the
family is around him. No text, no prompt, no new mode.

Do not add a stinger. Do not add a second ending. Let it be over.

### OPEN QUESTION: does the ending say goodbye?

Now that the missing goodbye is the wound, the ending has to decide whether to
close it, and this is not a call the design should make on its own.

**A. They stay.** The five of them walk the field together and nobody leaves. It
is dog heaven and he is home. Warm, uncomplicated, and it does not quite address
the goodbye.

**B. They say it.** They walk him across the field and then they let him go, and
he goes on ahead, and it is all right this time because everybody got to say it.
True to the premise, and it asks the player to lose him a second time.

**C. They say it, and then nobody has to leave.** The goodbye happens, the missing
thing gets filled in, and then they stay anyway, because this is not a place where
leaving is required. Completion without a second loss.

**Working default is C** until told otherwise. It is the only one that both closes
the wound and does not reopen it, and it is the version I would want to be
holding at the end.

---

## 12. What the game must never show

Hard prohibitions. Everything here is implied by the world 4 arena and by
dialogue, and the implication is stronger than the depiction in every case.

- The death. Not in a flashback, not in a fade, not in silhouette.
- The body.
- A funeral scene, a graveside, or the family crying at one.
- A headstone, a grave marker, or any text on the ground.
- Anyone saying the word "died," "dead," or "heaven."
- Gumball realizing it out loud.

The whole thing runs on the player knowing something the main character does not.
The moment anyone in the game says it plainly, that gap collapses and the game
becomes ordinary.

## 13. Game Boy technical notes

Target: DMG (original Game Boy), MBC5, 512 KB. Runs on GBC and Super Game Boy
without needing to.

### Sprites
- 160x144, 4 shades, 8x8 background tiles.
- Hardware limit: **40 sprites total, 10 per scanline.** The per-scanline limit is
  the one that will actually bite you.
- Use **8x16 sprite mode** (LCDC bit 2). A 16x16 Gumball is 2 hardware sprites
  instead of 4.
- Budget: Gumball 2-4 sprites, at most 3 enemies at 2-4 each, and hold a reserve
  for pickups. Design so vertical stacking of enemies is rare.

### Bosses on DMG
This is the part that will humble you if you plan it late.

- **Draw the boss body in the background layer, not with sprites.** A big
  sprite-built object blows the 10-per-scanline limit instantly. Animate by
  rewriting BG tiles during VBlank and by scrolling.
- Sprites only for the small moving parts: shower head, vacuum nozzle, mower
  blade.
- The storm is the cheapest of the four. Lightning is a **palette flash** (write
  BGP for a few frames), rain is a scrolling 1bpp overlay, wind is a constant
  added to Gumball's velocity, and the mud is a movement multiplier on a tile
  property. Almost no tile budget.
- One boss tileset per world, swapped in when level 3 loads. Fits comfortably in
  512 KB.
- Three phases, one hit each. Do not build a health bar for them.

### Build the text engine first
The emotional payload is text, and it is now two lines per reunion instead of
one. A variable-width font, a typewriter reveal, a portrait window, and a simple
script format are the *first* thing to build. If the text engine is bad the game
is bad, no matter how the platforming feels.

### Audio
Four channels. Write **one** Gumball melody. Each world theme is a variation:
the Boy bright and fast, the Girl playful, the Mother slow and warm, the Father
sparse and low.
Each "good boy" motif is four or five notes of that melody. The finale is the
full arrangement, the first time you hear it whole.

Silence is a real instrument in this game. The reunion cut, the two seconds after
the unanswered bark, and the room tone in the last field are all written into the
audio, not accidents of it.

### Assembly vs C: an honest recommendation

- **If the goal is learning the hardware:** write it in RGBDS assembly. Twelve
  levels and four bosses is a lot for a first Game Boy project, so be willing to
  cut a world down to two levels if month four arrives and you are not halfway.
- **If the goal is finishing the memorial:** use GBDK-2020 (C) for game logic and
  hand-write only the renderer, the scroll, and the collision in ASM. Several
  times faster, and the parts that need to be tight still will be.

Prototype **1-1 and the bite mechanic** first. If biting is not fun the structure
needs rethinking and you want to know in week two. Prototype **the reunion**
second, with programmer art, because it is the thing the entire game is for.

| Piece | Count |
|---|---|
| Title screen (two states) | 1 |
| Opening field | 1 map |
| Worlds | 4 |
| Levels per world | 3 |
| World levels | 12 maps |
| Finale walk | 1 map |
| **Total playable maps** | **14** |
| Bosses | 4 |
| Playable flashbacks | 4 (one per world, in level 2) |
| Reunions | 4, two lines each |
| Music tracks | 6 themes + 4 "good boy" motifs |
| Black cat scenes | 8 + 1 resolution |

Ship a vertical slice of World 1 first: all three levels, the bath, and the
reunion with both lines. Everything after is a repeat of a proven shape with
different art.

## 15. Risks worth naming

1. **The first 80% has to be fun without the twist.** Guilt is not enjoyable for
   three hours on its own. The verbs carry it, which is why each world gets a
   distinct mechanic rather than a distinct coat of paint.
2. **Four bosses is where a first Game Boy project dies.** Build the bath as a
   background-layer object early and prove the technique before designing the
   other three around it.
3. **The cat can become preachy.** Two appearances per world, hard cap, each
   shorter than the last. If a line does not make the player wince, cut it.
4. **Sentiment can outrun the game.** Resist having anyone state the theme. The
   idle sprite, the shrinking shy-away, the growing carrot bar, and the flowers
   over the soil say all of it without a word.
5. **Do not let the player fail a forgiveness.** No family member ever withholds
   it, and nothing can kill you during a reunion walk. The tension is Gumball's
   fear, not an actual risk.
6. **The second line in each reunion is the hardest writing in the project.** It
   has to be plain, short, and never poetic. These are people talking about the
   worst morning of a year, and people are bad at that on purpose. Write them
   underwritten.
